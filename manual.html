Using READY-Prompt

A Commodore 64 in your browser, with its own BASIC, a Datasette, two 1541 drives, the cartridge port and a modem. Everything you load stays in this browser for next time.

1Getting started

  1. Open the page and press a key or click the screen: the C64 switches on (browsers only allow sound after that). Tick Power on by itself in the Machine panel to skip this next time.
  2. It starts as a C64 did: **** COMMODORE 64 BASIC V2 ****, 38911 BASIC BYTES FREE and READY. Type PRINT "HELLO" and press Enter.
  3. Drop a program, tape, disk or cartridge anywhere on the page (or inside a .zip): it loads and runs.

The panels

  • Machine (left): power, reset, the model, speed, tape options, save states, the listing dialog.
  • Centre: the monitor, then the program slot, the cartridge port, the Datasette and drives 8 and 9, the on-screen keyboard and the shelf.
  • Settings (right): display, sound and the SID chip, the modem, the AI board, the joystick.

2The machine

PAL is the European C64: 50 frames a second, 985 kHz, 312 lines. NTSC is the American one: 60 frames, 1.02 MHz, 263 lines. Most European games and demos expect PAL; a few American games run only on NTSC. Changing it restarts the C64.

The SID chip menu picks the 6581 of the first C64s or the 8580 of the C64C. Tunes written in the 1980s were written for the 6581; it also plays the sampled speech and drums that the 8580 almost silences. Speed runs the machine two or four times faster, without sound.

3Programs and listings

  • A .prg (or .p00) goes into memory at its own address when BASIC is ready, and READY-Prompt types RUN. It waits in the Program slot: ▶ RUN starts it again from a fresh machine, ⤓ downloads it.
  • ✎ Listing takes a BASIC program typed or pasted in, as a magazine printed it, crunches it as the C64's own editor would and runs it. Magazine control codes in braces work: {CLR}, {HOME}, {3 DOWN}, {RED}, {RVS ON}.
  • Pages can link to a program: ?prg=, ?d64=, ?tap=, ?crt= or ?file= followed by its address loads it on arrival, if the site it is on allows other pages to fetch its files.

4Keyboard and joystick

The PC keyboard types by character, the way the C64 does it: " is SHIFT + 2 on a C64, and READY-Prompt presses that for you; @, *, +, : and = are keys of their own. Letters come out in capitals, as on the C64; SHIFT with a letter gives the graphics printed on the key's front.

⌨ Keyboard shows the C64's own keyboard. SHIFT, C= and CTRL stay down until the next key, so one finger can type CTRL + 1 (black) or C= + a letter (its graphic).

Most games read the joystick in port 2. With a joystick plugged in (the Joystick menu), the arrow keys and Right Ctrl (fire) are the stick, and so is a gamepad. Choose None to get the arrow keys back as cursor keys.

Keys

  • Esc: RUN/STOP. PgUp: RESTORE (RUN/STOP + RESTORE resets the screen).
  • Home: CLR/HOME. Backspace: INST/DEL.
  • Ctrl, Tab: CTRL. Alt: the Commodore key.
  • F1-F8: the C64's function keys.
  • F9 / F10: quick save / load. PgDn: rewind.

5Tapes

A .tap is a recording of the tape, pulse by pulse; a .t64 holds files. Either goes in the Datasette, and ▶ LOAD resets, presses PLAY and types LOAD and RUN.

  • With Load tapes at once, a file in the KERNAL's own format loads instantly. Anything else (games with a turbo loader, which is most of them) plays in real time, as the tape did; Fast-forward while a tape plays runs the C64 as fast as your computer allows meanwhile.
  • Untick Load tapes at once to hear and see the real thing: PRESS PLAY ON TAPE, FOUND, the screen blanking.
  • SAVE "NAME" records onto the tape in the deck (＋ Blank inserts an empty one); ⤓ downloads it as a .tap.

6Disks

A .d64 is a 1541 floppy disk. Dropped on the page it goes in drive 8, and READY-Prompt types LOAD"*",8,1 and RUN. $ lists the directory (LOAD"$",8, then LIST). Drive 9 takes a second disk.

  • BASIC talks to the drive as to a 1541: LOAD"NAME",8, SAVE"NAME",8, SAVE"@0:NAME",8 to replace a file, OPEN 2,8,2,"DATA,S,W" and PRINT#2 for a sequential file.
  • Channel 15 takes commands and gives the drive's error message: OPEN 15,8,15,"S:OLD" scratches a file, "R:NEW=OLD" renames one, "N:NAME,ID" formats the disk; INPUT#15,E,E$ reads the message.
  • ＋ Blank inserts a formatted disk; ⤓ downloads the disk with everything written to it.
  • Drive 8 (Machine panel) picks the drive. Fast loads at once but only answers the KERNAL. 1541-II and 1541 are the real drives: their own 6502 runs Commodore's DOS and the disk turns under the head, so games with fast loaders load, at the real speed (the page fast-forwards while the drive spins; uncheck it to hear the music of a loader). The drive light is the real one.

7Cartridges

A .crt goes in the cartridge port and the C64 restarts with it, as it did when you plugged one in with the power off. READY-Prompt runs normal 8K and 16K cartridges, Ultimax ones, and the bank-switching kinds most games and demos came on: Ocean, Magic Desk, System 3, Dinamic, Fun Play, Simons' BASIC and EasyFlash. Freezers such as the Action Replay and the Final Cartridge are not there yet. ⏏ pulls the cartridge out.

8Save states

  • F9 (Quick save) keeps the whole machine as it is, in this browser; F10 (Quick load) goes back to it.
  • PgDn (Rewind) goes back a second; press it again for more, up to 30 seconds.
  • ⤓ State downloads the machine as a .rdy file; drop it on the page to go back to that moment. A state holds the machine, not the tape, disks or cartridge: load it with the same ones in place.

9The modem, TERM and the AI board

Tick SwiftLink modem to plug in a SwiftLink, the RS-232 cartridge the C64 terminals of the 1990s used (CCGMS, Novaterm), with a Hayes modem behind it that dials telnet BBSes. It sits in the cartridge port: plugging in a game takes it out.

Load TERM.prg from the shelf (it plugs the SwiftLink in by itself). ATI lists the boards, ATDT 1 dials the first. TERM starts in ANSI mode, for the PC boards; F1 switches to PETSCII, for the Commodore boards, marked (P). F7 goes back to BASIC with the call up, and SYS 2061 returns.

ATDT AI calls the AI board: chat with its sysop (DeepSeek) or with the Bastard Operator From Hell, or order a program in BASIC or machine code. The board tries it on a spare C64 and puts it on the disk in drive 8: press F7 and LOAD"NAME",8. It can also fix a BASIC program of yours: SAVE it to that disk first. It needs your own DeepSeek API key (the AI board panel says how to get one).

The modem's commands

  • ATI: the phone book. ATDT n, ATDT host:port: dial.
  • +++ (a second's pause either side): back to commands; ATH hangs up, ATO returns to the call.
  • ATM0 silences the speaker, ATL1-3 sets its volume, AT&W keeps them.

10What the browser keeps

Every file you load waits on the shelf; click one to load it again, ✕ to take it off. The tape in the Datasette, the disks in the drives (with what was written to them), the cartridge, the quick save and your settings come back on your next visit. All of it stays in this browser (IndexedDB and localStorage); nothing goes to a server, except BBS calls and the AI board's questions to DeepSeek.

11Where to find software

  • CSDb, the C64 Scene Database: demos, music collections, tools and new games from the scene, most as .prg or .d64.
  • The High Voltage SID Collection: the music, as .sid files (the players of which are C64 programs).
  • New commercial and free games are still made for the C64: itch.io lists many, often with a .d64 or .crt.
  • Old games are still under copyright, even when they are no longer sold: use copies you own, or ones their authors have released.

12When something does not work

  • A disk game stops at "LOADING" or crashes: it probably uses a fast loader that runs its own program on the 1541's CPU. Set Drive 8 to 1541-II and load it again. Copy protections that need the exact bits of the original disk will not pass: a .d64 keeps only the sectors.
  • A very old program behaves oddly: try KERNAL revision 1 or 2, the ones of the first C64s.
  • A game ignores the joystick: try port 1 in the Joystick menu.
  • The arrow keys move the stick instead of the cursor: set the joystick to None.
  • A game runs too fast or the picture rolls: it may be made for the other TV system: switch between PAL and NTSC.
  • No sound: click the page once (browsers need it), and check the volume.
  • The C64 is stuck: RUN/STOP + RESTORE (Esc + PgUp) returns to BASIC; Reset restarts it.
